Primary request: create a highly readable mid-season map made of regular hexagonal territorial segments. Two kingdoms already control substantial territory but do not touch each other anywhere; a wide continuous belt of neutral hexes remains between them.
Composition/framing: wide 16:9 landscape. Near-top-down orthographic view with only a slight three-quarter tilt, approximately 70–75 degrees above the ground. Show one finite irregularly shaped inland region assembled from about 30 large equal flat-top hexagonal cells in staggered rows. The hex grid is the dominant readable structure.
Territory layout: the western kingdom controls a compact connected cluster of about 8–10 hexes on the left. The eastern kingdom controls a separate compact connected cluster of about 8–10 hexes on the right. Between them is an unbroken north-to-south neutral belt two or three full hexes wide. Red and blue controlled hexes must never share an edge and must not touch at a corner. Neutral hexes fully separate the kingdoms everywhere.
Visual treatment of ownership: one kingdom uses a restrained terracotta/rust tint, the other a restrained slate-blue tint. Neutral cells keep natural olive-green and earth-brown terrain. Apply color as a subtle clean strategic-map surface tint that still preserves terrain. Each hex has one crisp thin dark-neutral outline; no walls, fences, cliffs, gaps or raised tile edges on territorial borders. The terrain itself remains continuous beneath the map overlay.
Terrain and development: use only simple low-poly terrain masses: sparse conifer groups, open grass, low hills, one clean winding river and a few dirt roads. Every controlled hex contains one small simplified physical town-hall marker: a modest low-poly timber-and-stone civic building with a tiny faction banner. A few interior controlled hexes may also contain one tiny farm, mill or logging site, but keep the map sparse. Neutral hexes contain wilderness and perhaps one tiny ruin, but no active town halls or faction banners. Roads within each kingdom connect several town halls and approach the neutral belt without joining the opponent's road network.
Lighting/mood: clear soft daylight, low contrast, calm anticipation before first territorial contact, clean shapes and generous visual breathing room.
Hierarchy: first read the hex grid, then the two separated colored territory masses, then the sparse roads and town-hall markers. Everything must remain legible at thumbnail size.
Constraints: clean low-poly game visual language; simple and diagrammatically readable while still depicting a continuous physical world. This is not a painted fantasy landscape and not a realistic satellite map.
